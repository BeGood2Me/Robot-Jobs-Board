Robotics headlines swing between humanoid demos and warehouse AMR rollouts. The quieter signal is the text of live job posts: which robots, which languages, which cities. Robot Jobs Board classifies those posts with a keyword taxonomy, so this piece is a snapshot of what the board is actually asking for, not a funding narrative.

Use it as a map. Candidates can line up a project with [C++ jobs](/skills/cpp-jobs) or [ROS 2 jobs](/skills/ros2-jobs). Employers can see how crowded a niche is before they [write the next requisition](/guides/how-to-write-robotics-job-descriptions).

## Robotics domains that keep showing up

**Humanoids.** Labs in San Jose, Austin, and a handful of other cities hire perception, whole-body control, data operations, and on-site operators. The software bar is high. The operator and deployment bar is "will you show up to the cell." Browse [humanoid jobs](/robots/humanoid-jobs) and company pages such as [Figure](/companies/figure) and [Apptronik](/companies/apptronik).

**AMRs and warehouse robotics.** Titles talk about deployment, fleet software, and navigation. The work is less glamorous and more hired. If you want volume at junior and mid level, start with [AMR jobs](/robots/amr-jobs).

**Drones and aerial autonomy.** Defense-adjacent companies still hire C++ and PX4 people in volume. See [drone jobs](/robots/drone-jobs) and [PX4 jobs](/skills/px4-jobs). Clearance language is common. Remote is less common than software Twitter suggests.

**Industrial and field.** Cobots, PLC, inspection quadrupeds, and outdoor robots show up as applications engineering and field service as much as core autonomy. [Industrial](/robots/industrial-jobs) and [field](/robots/field-jobs) filters catch them.

**Medical.** Smaller counts, higher specialization, longer hiring cycles. [Medical robotics jobs](/robots/medical-jobs) are worth a dedicated search if you already know that world.

## Skills the taxonomy keeps tagging

Across descriptions, the durable cluster is still **C++, Python, Linux**. ROS 2 appears whenever the company did not invent a private middleware. CUDA and PyTorch cluster in perception and humanoid policy jobs. PLC remains the secret handshake for factory integration. Isaac Sim and Gazebo appear when the team cannot put every new hire on a scarce robot.

If you can only practice two things this quarter, practice C++ on Linux and one simulator. That pair covers more live postings than a third web framework.

Seniority language is noisy. "Staff" and "principal" in humanoid labs often mean research plus production. "Senior" in AMR deployment can mean you have commissioned a site. Read the bullets, not the level. Our classifier maps staff to the principal bucket so search stays coarse on purpose. Filter [senior](/?seniority=senior) and [lead](/?seniority=lead) separately when you care.

## Location patterns in robotics hiring

On-site still wins for hardware. San Jose, Austin, Costa Mesa, Boston, and Seattle appear because that is where labs and defense programs sit. Remote listings exist, mostly for fleet software and tooling, and they usually still want overlap with US hours. Combination pages such as [remote humanoid jobs](/locations/remote/humanoid-jobs) are the ones to watch as volume grows.

The United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and specific European sites show up around companies, not as a generic "work from anywhere" market. If a city only has two jobs, we keep the page live but we do not index it until it crosses five jobs. That is how we avoid thin location spam.

## How to read a robotics job posting

Ignore the first adjective. Search the body for:

- The robot noun.
- The middleware (ROS 2, PX4, or unnamed).
- Whether travel or a customer floor is required.
- The first ninety-day outcome.

If those four are missing, the company has not decided what the job is. Candidates should skip it. Employers should [fix the JD](/guides/how-to-write-robotics-job-descriptions) before boosting the listing.

## What this is not

This is not a salary survey. Bands move too fast and too few posts include numbers. It is also not a claim that humanoids have "won." Warehouse AMRs still hire the people who keep robots moving at 2 a.m.

The useful move is to pick a domain, pick a stack, and watch the [job list](/) weekly. The taxonomy will keep tagging what the market writes down, which is more honest than the keynote.
